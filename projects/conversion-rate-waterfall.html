Year-over-year conversion rate waterfall

Decomposed a large seasonal attach-rate improvement into three explainable drivers—experience optimization, funnel routing, and customer mix—using counterfactuals, Kitagawa decomposition, and a leadership-ready waterfall that reconciles to the official scorecard definition.

Business question

At end of season, leadership saw attach rate (eligible completers who took an optional paid add-on) jump year-over-year while eligible volume stayed nearly flat. The ask: quantify the total delta, attribute it to stakeholder-recognizable drivers, and separate durable product gains from one-time environmental effects.

Metric contract (define once)

Before decomposition, the ratio was locked to the executive scorecard:

ComponentDefinition
NumeratorDistinct customers with completed attach per governed flag—not funnel “select” proxies
DenominatorDistinct eligible completers (same eligibility rules both years)
WindowFull season through filing deadline; day-of-week alignment for YoY compares
GrainCustomer level—never session-level for attach

Three-driver waterfall

Raw counterfactual lifts often sum to more than the observed delta because drivers overlap (better UX × more people seeing the monetization surface). I used transparent proportional allocation so bars reconcile exactly to the official YoY change.

Illustrative chart only — rounded placeholder values (12% → 17%, +5pp) demonstrate methodology and layout. Production analysis used scorecard-anchored rates and driver shares from counterfactual models below.

Driver Mechanism Method
Experience optimization Winning landing-page variant live full current season; partial prior season after mid-season ramp Experiment lift ratio × pre-ramp volume counterfactual
Bypass flow removal Prior-season shortcut skipped monetization surface (~3% view rate vs ~30% standard path) Apply standard-path attach rate to shortcut population
Value-bucket mix Average customer value shifted toward higher-propensity segments Kitagawa mix effect (rate effect corroborates UX driver)

Driver A — Experiment counterfactual + ramp timing

Prior-season A/B test gave a relative lift ratio R = treatment rate ÷ control rate among the same eligible population. Treatment ramped mid-season; current year ran treatment from day one.

  • Split prior year into pre-ramp (mostly control UX) and post-ramp volume.
  • Counterfactual pre-ramp rate: r′_pre = r_pre × R.
  • Volume-weighted prior-year counterfactual blends pre and post segments.
  • Raw UX lift = counterfactual minus actual prior-year rate.

Validation signature: YoY weekly rates showed a wide gap before the aligned ramp week, then collapsed to ~1–2pp once both years had the winning variant—exactly what a single dominant treatment produces. Current-year micro-tests near flat index confirmed incremental tweaks were “inches,” not the YoY story.

Driver B — Shortcut / bypass path

A minority of prior-season completers used a shortcut checkout that bypassed the monetization landing page. Attach rate and surface view rate were both materially lower on that path. Shortcut was removed current season.

Counterfactual: if shortcut users had followed the standard path at the standard-path attach rate, compute incremental attaches and convert to percentage points on the blended prior-year rate.

Driver C — Kitagawa on value buckets

Attach propensity rises with a customer value proxy (refund size, order value, etc.). Kitagawa decomposition separates:

EffectInterpretation
Rate effectWithin-bucket attach change (uniform lift → UX story)
Mix effectComposition shift into higher-propensity buckets (waterfall driver)
InteractionJoint movement of mix and rate

For the bridge, only the mix portion was allocated as Driver C so UX (Driver A) was not double-counted.

Proportional allocation

Each driver’s share of the observed delta:

Δᵢ = Δ_obs × (Δᵢ_raw / Σ Δ_raw)

Preserves rank order, sums to 100%, and documents raw lifts in appendix for finance reconciliation— no opaque regression residual.

Portable lessons

  • Lock the ratio before debating drivers—days are lost to numerator/denominator drift.
  • One large experiment + ramp timing can explain most of a YoY rate story; validate with weekly DOW alignment.
  • Always check surface view rate on shortcut paths—not attach rate alone.
  • Kitagawa separates mix from product; uniform bucket rate lifts point to UX, not demographics.
  • Ship attach-rate monitoring on every live monetization surface—silent outages are expensive.
